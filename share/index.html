Terms, expected tables, and how they connect — so the business and the product stay on the same page.

Tables and links below are expected, not a locked schema. Product columns, factory Excel maps, and words like “vendor” / “SKU” wait on David’s lists. Do not treat extra fields on this page as requirements.

What the business does

  • Buys product from factories using purchase orders (Excel sent to the factory).
  • Sells wholesale to customers (other businesses) via a shop, or staff place the same kind of sales order for them.
  • Staff run catalog, purchasing, stock, customers, and billing in one back office. Today that back office is SoloVue.
  • A sales order is confirmed as the order page plus an email that matches that page.
  • Customers may pay on terms (Net 30 / 60 / 90). When due, they get a statement email — not a marketing blast.
  • Shopify is retail. It is a separate channel, not this wholesale product.

Defined terms

Only words we can stand behind today. Source: CONTEXT.md in the product repo.

Customer
A wholesale business that buys from us. Orders on the shop, or staff place the same kind of order for them. Not: vendor, client, account (until the business locks those words).
Staff
An employee of David Christopher’s who uses the back office. Not: “admin” for every internal user.
Factory
A business we buy product from. We send them purchase orders. Not: vendor / supplier until the glossary picks one word.
Sales order
An order a customer places with us. Confirmation = page + matching email. Includes ship-to and, later, tracking. Not a purchase order or invoice.
Purchase order
An order staff build to buy from a factory. Today: export Excel, fix layout, send to the factory. Not a customer order.
Invoice
A bill we send a customer for a sales order. Some customers have time to pay. Not a statement.
Statement
An email that a customer owes a stated amount because payment terms came due. Not the invoice itself, not a newsletter.
Item number
The identifier staff type to find a product when building a purchase order. Not locked as “SKU” until the business says they are the same.

How the tables connect
  • A customer does not contain orders. A sales order points at the customer.
  • A product does not store “how many we have.” stock_movement does; stock_on_hand is the running picture.
  • Purchase-order lines and sales-order lines copy what they need from the product when the line is added, so later catalog edits do not rewrite history.
  • An invoice points at a sales order and a customer. A statement is not a second invoice — it is the “this is now due” notice.
